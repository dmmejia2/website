About

Assistant Professor of Instruction · UTEP Computer Science

Born and raised in El Paso. B.S., M.S., and Ph.D. in Computer Science, all at UTEP.

I am Assistant Professor of Instruction in the Department of Computer Science at The University of Texas at El Paso, where I completed my entire academic training. I am also Program Director for the M.S. in Software Engineering (MSSwE) and Education, Workforce & Professional Development Lead for the Institute for Applied AI Innovation (AAII), including the university-wide AI Champions faculty program under an NSF Transformation Grant. Since July 2025 I have served as CAHSI Lead for the GenAI in CS Education Consortium.

Since Fall 2019 I have taught ten distinct courses from first-year computing through the graduate practicum. Course development includes Applied Agile Software Engineering and Fundamentals of Financial Literacy (with Visa), alongside industry partnerships with Google. Published scholarship covers agile software engineering pedagogy and generative AI in CS education (SIGCSE, ITiCSE), with earlier work in knowledge graphs and smart mobility. I am co-PI on NSF S-STEM (~$4.98M) and was PI on Google exploreCSR and TensorFlow awards.

Department and program roles include MSSwE program direction, TA / IA / Peer Leader coordination, Fast Track advising, CodePath liaison work, and graduate student coordination, aimed at giving students genuine access to an excellent computer science education.

Teaching philosophy

Many of my students come from backgrounds and a culture similar to my own. Born and raised in El Paso and educated entirely at UTEP, I know first-hand what it is like to enter a first computer science class knowing nothing about the field, and to still feel there is more to learn upon completing a graduate degree.

I do not define student success narrowly. For one student, success is understanding a difficult topic for the first time. For another, it is landing an internship, publishing a research paper, earning a scholarship, graduating, being accepted to graduate school, or finding a position at a company where they are genuinely happy. There is no single template. My role is to meet students where they are and help them grow from that point, not to push every student toward the same outcome.

Two components sit at the core of how I teach: engagement and humanity. Engagement keeps students curious through activities, delivery, and sustained interaction with the material. Humanity means letting students see that I am still learning and growing alongside them. Students arrive with different preparation levels and ways of learning, and I incorporate multiple modalities into instruction: live coding, visuals, problem solving, projects, and recorded lecture walkthroughs on Blackboard. Closing the gap between classroom preparation and industry expectation is central to courses such as Applied Agile Software Engineering and to partnerships with Google and Visa.

Approach

My leadership grew out of a straightforward instinct: when something needs work, I want to fix it, and I want it to run efficiently once fixed. That instinct took me from the classroom into Program Director of the MSSwE program, TA and IA Coordinator, CAHSI Lead for the GenAI in CS Education Consortium, and CodePath Institutional Liaison, among other roles. Each serves the same population of students I have taught for years, aimed at giving twenty-first-century students genuine access to an excellent computer science education, not just a seat in a classroom.
